Sharing in Oculon works best when you choose the smallest surface that still supports the work.

Item sharing is separate from workspace membership and from Space organization. Someone can be in the workspace and still need explicit access to a model, board, view, or other resource.

## Choose the right thing to share

- Share a **model** when someone needs inputs, drafts, or planning logic.
- Share a **view** when someone needs a filtered slice of a model or Transactions.
- Share a **board** when someone needs a decision-ready presentation.

If stakeholders only need filtered detail, start with a view. If they mainly need curated presentation, start with a board. Share the full model only when they also need the underlying planning surface.

## Item access levels

Oculon item access commonly uses:

| Access level | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **View** | Read-only access to what is shared |
| **Edit** | Change content without taking over permission management |
| **Full Access** | Structural changes, sharing, and permission settings on that item |
| **No Access** | Explicitly remove access |

For deeper role tables and inheritance explanations, see [Roles and permissions](/concepts/workspace-and-collaboration/sharing-and-permissions/roles-and-permissions) and [Permission inheritance](/concepts/workspace-and-collaboration/sharing-and-permissions/permission-inheritance).

## What sharing does not change

Sharing an item does not automatically expand access to everything behind it.

- A view narrows access. It does not widen underlying source permissions.
- A board can present data from several sources without elevating access to those sources.
- Effective access is the minimum of the shared item access and the source access the person already has.

## Validate the share before you send it

1. Confirm the person can access the workspace and Space where the item lives.
2. Confirm the item is shared at the right level for that audience.
3. Check whether the item depends on restricted models, Transactions, views, or other sources.
4. Confirm what the recipient can do after opening it: view, edit, or manage.

## Watch for dependency gaps

Missing source access usually shows up as hidden blocks, incomplete data, or a request-access state. Review both the shared item and the underlying sources before you resend the link.
