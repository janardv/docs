Time aggregation defines how a metric should behave when you review it at a coarser time level. It answers a business question: once several periods are grouped together, what number should represent that group?

## Why it matters

Not every metric should behave the same way in a quarter or year summary.

- A revenue line often needs a summed total.
- A headcount line often needs an end-of-period view.
- A percentage or rate often needs to be recalculated or weighted, not summed.

If the higher-level value is wrong, users lose trust quickly even when the monthly data is correct.

## Aggregation versus rollups

It helps to separate two related concepts:

- [`Time rollups`](/docs-new/time/time-rollups) decide which lower-level periods belong together.
- `Time aggregation` decides what value should represent that grouped result.

For example, a quarter may roll up three months. Aggregation decides whether the quarter should show the sum of those months, the ending value, or another business-appropriate interpretation.

## Common aggregation patterns

Use the business meaning of the metric to guide the rule:

- `Flow metrics` such as revenue, expense, and units sold often make sense as sums.
- `Point-in-time metrics` such as end-of-month headcount, cash balance, or open pipeline often make more sense as an ending value.
- `Rates and percentages` often need a weighted or recomputed result instead of a simple period sum.

The right answer is not "the default." The right answer is the one a finance reviewer would expect to see in the summary.

## How to choose the right behavior

Ask these questions for each important metric:

- Is this metric additive across time?
- Is this metric a balance at a point in time?
- Would a reviewer expect a period-end number or a blended number?
- Does the quarter or year view tell the same story as the monthly view?

If you cannot explain the quarter total in plain language, the aggregation approach probably needs more work.

## Examples

### Revenue

Revenue is usually a flow metric, so quarter and year views typically sum the lower-level periods.

### Headcount

Headcount often behaves like an end-of-period metric. A yearly summary usually needs to show the closing value or another clearly defined business interpretation, not the sum of all months.

### Margin percentage

A margin percentage usually should not be summed across periods. Review whether the summary should be recalculated from the underlying values instead.

## Troubleshooting a wrong quarter or year total

If a higher-level value looks wrong, check these in order:

1. confirm the metric's business meaning
2. confirm the grouped periods are correct in the rollup
3. review whether the actual and planned formulas behave differently around the cutoff
4. recheck any dimensions or filters applied in the view
