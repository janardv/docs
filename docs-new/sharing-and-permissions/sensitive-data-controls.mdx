Sensitive planning data needs more than a private link. It needs narrow sharing, predictable visibility rules, and repeatable review.

## Start with least privilege

The safest default is simple: give people the smallest access scope that still lets them do their job.

That usually means:

- sharing the summary surface before the underlying model
- limiting access to the exact audience that needs it
- keeping `Full access` restricted to a small set of accountable owners

If someone only needs the outcome, do not start by giving access to the full planning surface.

## Use the right controls together

Sensitive data protection usually depends on several controls working together:

- least-privilege permissions
- restricted teamspaces or groups
- limited sharing of underlying models and source data
- visibility controls that reduce exposure during screen sharing or live review
- audit and review habits for high-risk content

One control is rarely enough on its own.

## Design for the audience

Not everyone needs the same level of detail.

- Share a board or page when the audience needs a decision-ready summary.
- Share a view when the audience needs one controlled slice.
- Share the full model only when the audience also needs to inspect or edit the planning logic.

This keeps sensitive data from spreading more broadly than the workflow requires.

## Recheck after changes

Sensitive-data risk often appears after the original setup.

Review your assumptions again when:

- content moves between teamspaces or owners
- new groups are added
- a board starts pulling from additional sources
- an executive or external review process begins

The biggest mistakes usually happen during reorganization, not during first creation.
