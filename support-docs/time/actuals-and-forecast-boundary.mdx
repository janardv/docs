The actuals and forecast boundary is the point where historical values stop and planning logic starts. In Oculon, this is usually controlled by the last actual date.

## Why the boundary matters

A clear boundary helps you avoid mixing observed results with projected values. It also makes scenario comparisons easier because everyone is working from the same baseline.

## What should happen at the boundary

- periods before the boundary should remain actuals
- periods after the boundary should follow forecast logic
- formulas that depend on actuals should respond consistently around the cutoff

## Review it regularly

Update the boundary when a new actual period closes. If you forget, forecasts may continue too far into history or actuals may fail to flow into the model.
